How WebM Encodes 10-Bit and 12-Bit VP9 Video

WebM supports high dynamic range (HDR) and wide color gamuts by utilizing the VP9 video codec's advanced profiles designed for 10-bit and 12-bit color depths. This article explains how VP9 allocates bit depth through specific profile architectures, manages increased mathematical precision during quantization and transformation, and stores color metadata within the WebM container to accurately render deep color content.

VP9 Deep Color Profiles

VP9 manages different bit depths and chroma subsampling formats using designated profiles. While Profiles 0 and 1 are restricted to standard 8-bit video, Profiles 2 and 3 are dedicated to deep color:

  • Profile 2: Supports 10-bit and 12-bit color depth using 4:2:0 chroma subsampling. This is the standard profile used for consumer HDR video distribution, such as 4K HDR streaming.
  • Profile 3: Supports 10-bit and 12-bit color depth with advanced chroma formats, including 4:2:2, 4:4:0, and 4:4:4 (full chroma resolution), as well as non-subsampled RGB color representation.

Frame Header Signaling

When encoding 10-bit or 12-bit video, VP9 specifies the color depth in the uncompressed header of each keyframe and intra-only frame. The encoder writes specific bit markers to declare the profile (Profile 2 or 3) and sets the bit_depth syntax element:

  • A dedicated flag signals whether the stream uses standard 8-bit or higher precision.
  • If deep color is active, an additional bit distinguishes between 10-bit and 12-bit precision.
  • The header also signals the color space, transfer characteristics (such as SMPTE ST 2084 / PQ or HLG), and color primaries (such as BT.2020).

Internal Transform and Quantization Scaling

Deep color encoding changes how the VP9 compression pipeline handles pixel arithmetic. Moving from 8-bit (256 color levels per channel) to 10-bit (1,024 levels) or 12-bit (4,096 levels) expands the dynamic range and reduces color banding artifacts.

  1. Dynamic Range of Transforms: VP9 applies discrete cosine transforms (DCT) and asymmetric discrete sine transforms (ADST) with higher-precision integer arithmetic to avoid rounding errors when compressing 10-bit or 12-bit sample values.
  2. Quantization Tables: Quantization parameters (\(Q\)-indices) are scaled to match the expanded dynamic range. The encoder uses wider bit-width registers to hold quantized transform coefficients, ensuring subtle gradients in dark and bright regions are preserved.
  3. Loop Filtering: In-loop deblocking filters and sub-pixel interpolation algorithms adapt their threshold parameters based on the 10-bit or 12-bit range to prevent smoothing out genuine micro-details while eliminating compression artifacts.

Container-Level Metadata in WebM

The WebM container, based on a subset of the Matroska (EBML) specification, carries essential deep color and HDR metadata in the track header elements alongside the VP9 bitstream:

  • BitsPerChannel: Explicitly set to 10 or 12 in the track video configuration.
  • Colour Element: Encapsulates the specific color parameters required by display hardware:
    • MatrixCoefficients (e.g., ITU-R BT.2020 non-constant luminance).
    • TransferCharacteristics (e.g., Perceptual Quantizer or Hybrid Log-Gamma).
    • Primaries (e.g., BT.2020 or DCI-P3).
    • MasteringMetadata: Contains the chromaticity coordinates for the red, green, and blue mastering primaries, white point, and minimum/maximum luminance values.

During playback, the decoding pipeline reads the WebM container elements to configure the video output pipeline and GPU color management, while the VP9 decoder processes the high bit-depth frames according to the signaled profile.
