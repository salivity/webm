YUV to RGB Matrix Transformations in WebM

This article provides an overview of the mathematical matrix transformations used to convert YUV (specifically YCbCr) video data into the RGB color space during WebM video playback. WebM containers utilizing VP8, VP9, or AV1 codecs store video frames in planar YUV formats to optimize compression efficiency. Rendering these frames onto computer displays requires decoders and graphics rendering pipelines to perform precise affine transformations and color space conversion matrices defined by standards such as ITU-R BT.601 and ITU-R BT.709.

The YUV to RGB Conversion Pipeline

Display devices operate in the RGB color model, whereas WebM video streams are typically encoded in \(\text{YUV420p}\) (\(\text{YCbCr}\) with 4:2:0 chroma subsampling). The conversion process consists of three sequential operations:

  1. Chroma Upsampling: Reconstructing full-resolution chroma channels (\(U/Cb\) and \(V/Cr\)) from subsampled planes.
  2. Offset and Range Scaling: Adjusting digital quantization levels based on whether the stream uses limited range (studio swing) or full range (full swing).
  3. Linear Matrix Transformation: Multiplying the normalized YUV vector by a \(3 \times 3\) conversion matrix to derive red, green, and blue values.
  4. Clamping: Restricting the final RGB outputs to valid display intervals (e.g., \(0\) to \(255\) for 8-bit color).

Offset Adjustments (Limited vs. Full Range)

Most WebM videos use limited-range (standard) video quantization. In an 8-bit limited-range representation:

  • \(Y\) (Luma) has a valid range of \(16\) to \(235\).
  • \(U\) and \(V\) (Chroma components) have a valid range of \(16\) to \(240\), with an achromatic center at \(128\).

Before matrix multiplication, these components are shifted to remove offsets:

\[Y' = Y - 16\] \[U' = U - 128\] \[V' = V - 128\]

For full-range video (where \(Y\), \(U\), and \(V\) span \(0\) to \(255\)), only the chroma channels are centered:

\[Y' = Y\] \[U' = U - 128\] \[V' = V - 128\]

Color Space Matrices

The WebM container metadata specifies the color primaries and matrix coefficients. The two most common standards are ITU-R BT.601 and ITU-R BT.709.

1. ITU-R BT.601 (Standard Definition)

BT.601 is commonly used for standard-definition video and is the default color space for legacy VP8 streams.

For limited-range 8-bit inputs, the matrix equation is:

\[\begin{bmatrix} R \\ G \\ B \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.164383 & 0.000000 & 1.596027 \\ 1.164383 & -0.391762 & -0.812968 \\ 1.164383 & 2.017232 & 0.000000 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} Y - 16 \\ U - 128 \\ V - 128 \end{bmatrix}\]

Expressed as individual equations:

  • \(R = 1.164 \times (Y - 16) + 1.596 \times (V - 128)\)
  • \(G = 1.164 \times (Y - 16) - 0.392 \times (U - 128) - 0.813 \times (V - 128)\)
  • \(B = 1.164 \times (Y - 16) + 2.017 \times (U - 128)\)

For full-range BT.601:

\[\begin{bmatrix} R \\ G \\ B \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.000000 & 0.000000 & 1.402000 \\ 1.000000 & -0.344136 & -0.714136 \\ 1.000000 & 1.772000 & 0.000000 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} Y \\ U - 128 \\ V - 128 \end{bmatrix}\]

2. ITU-R BT.709 (High Definition)

BT.709 is the standard for high-definition (720p, 1080p) WebM content, widely used in VP9 and AV1 encodes.

For limited-range 8-bit inputs, the transformation matrix is:

\[\begin{bmatrix} R \\ G \\ B \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.164383 & 0.000000 & 1.792741 \\ 1.164383 & -0.213249 & -0.532909 \\ 1.164383 & 2.112402 & 0.000000 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} Y - 16 \\ U - 128 \\ V - 128 \end{bmatrix}\]

Expressed as individual equations:

  • \(R = 1.164 \times (Y - 16) + 1.793 \times (V - 128)\)
  • \(G = 1.164 \times (Y - 16) - 0.213 \times (U - 128) - 0.533 \times (V - 128)\)
  • \(B = 1.164 \times (Y - 16) + 2.112 \times (U - 128)\)

For full-range BT.709:

\[\begin{bmatrix} R \\ G \\ B \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.000000 & 0.000000 & 1.574800 \\ 1.000000 & -0.187324 & -0.468124 \\ 1.000000 & 1.855600 & 0.000000 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} Y \\ U - 128 \\ V - 128 \end{bmatrix}\]

3. ITU-R BT.2020 (Ultra High Definition / HDR)

For 4K, 8K, or HDR WebM streams encoded in 10-bit or 12-bit VP9/AV1, BT.2020 coefficients are applied. For 10-bit limited-range video (where offsets scale to \(Y - 64\) and \(UV - 512\)):

\[\begin{bmatrix} R \\ G \\ B \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1.164384 & 0.000000 & 1.678674 \\ 1.164384 & -0.187326 & -0.650424 \\ 1.164384 & 2.141772 & 0.000000 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} Y - 64 \\ U - 512 \\ V - 512 \end{bmatrix}\]

Implementation in Web Playback

During browser-based WebM playback (via HTML5 <video> or WebCodecs), these matrix multiplications are typically executed on the GPU using fragment shaders. Normalized texture coordinates \([0.0, 1.0]\) are fed into a \(4 \times 4\) affine transformation matrix that simultaneously handles offset subtraction, range scaling, and color space conversion in a single matrix-vector multiplication pass.
