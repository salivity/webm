How WebM Opus Reconstructs Stereo Width

The WebM Opus audio decoder reconstructs perceived stereo image width from mid-side (M/S) encoded signals by reversing psychoacoustic matrix operations and applying band-specific spatial rotation parameters. Within the Opus CELT (Constrained Energy Lapped Transform) layer, stereo audio is broken down into frequency bands where channel relationships are represented by normalized energy shapes and an inter-channel angle parameter. By decoding these energy envelopes, rotating the normalized vectors back from the mid/side coordinate space to the discrete left/right domain, and scaling them by their band energies, the decoder fully restores both the channel separation and the original stereo width without phase artifacts.

The Mid-Side Coordinate Transform

In the Opus codec, spatial redundancy between channels is compressed using mid-side processing. Rather than storing independent left (\(L\)) and right (\(R\)) channels, the encoder converts them into a sum (Mid, representing center content) and difference (Side, representing width and directional cues):

  • \(M = \frac{L + R}{\sqrt{2}}\)
  • \(S = \frac{L - R}{\sqrt{2}}\)

To reconstruct discrete channels, the decoder applies the inverse unmixing matrix:

  • \(L = \frac{M + S}{\sqrt{2}}\)
  • \(R = \frac{M - S}{\sqrt{2}}\)

However, simple sum-and-difference unmixing alone is insufficient for maintaining accurate stereo width across low bitrates, where quantization errors can cause phase cancellation or stereo collapse. Opus solves this by decoupling band energy from signal shape.

Spherical Vector Quantization and Stereo Angles

For each critical bark-scale frequency band, Opus treats the mid and side signals as coordinates on a 2D plane. Instead of independently quantizing the raw \(M\) and \(S\) amplitudes, the encoder computes:

  1. Total Band Energy: The combined RMS energy of the band.
  2. Stereo Angle (\(\theta\)): The angular relationship between the mid and side components, defined as \(\theta = \arctan(S / M)\).
  3. Normalized Shape Vectors: Unit-norm residual spectral shapes for both \(M\) and \(S\).

During playback, the WebM Opus decoder parses the bitstream to retrieve the quantized stereo angle \(\theta\) for each band. The angle directly dictates the stereo width:

  • \(\theta = 0\): Pure mid signal (complete mono, zero width).
  • \(\theta = \frac{\pi}{4}\): Equal mid and side energy (independent, uncorrelated left and right channels).
  • \(\theta = \frac{\pi}{2}\): Pure side signal (out-of-phase stereo or extreme width with no center image).

Reconstructing the Frequency Bins

The decoder performs band reconstruction by executing a trigonometric de-rotation of the decoded unit vectors:

  1. Weighting: The decoded mid shape vector is multiplied by \(\cos(\theta)\), while the side shape vector is multiplied by \(\sin(\theta)\).
  2. Energy Scaling: Both vectors are multiplied by the reconstructed band energy, ensuring the total spectral power matches the target envelope.
  3. Channel Rotation: The scaled mid and side vectors are mapped back to discrete channels using the inverse matrix operations:
    • \(X_L(k) = M(k)\cos(\theta) + S(k)\sin(\theta)\)
    • \(X_R(k) = M(k)\cos(\theta) - S(k)\sin(\theta)\)

This formulation guarantees that the energy sum of the reconstructed channels equals the original signal energy, preventing energy loss or artificial amplification during the spatial transformation.

Dynamic Band Coupling and Width Preservation

Stereo reconstruction in Opus adapts dynamically across frequency bands and frames. At high frequencies or low bitrates, bitstream budgets may not permit encoding independent shapes for both mid and side. In these scenarios, the decoder uses a technique called dual-stereo prediction:

  • The decoder synthesizes the side channel directly from the mid channel's decoded shape using a pseudo-random orthogonal vector.
  • The decoded stereo angle \(\theta\) is still applied, preserving the macroscopic stereo width and spatial spread even when the exact side-channel phase data has been discarded.

Additionally, Opus supports per-band switching between mid-side coding and discrete left-right coding. When channels are highly uncorrelated or out-of-phase—conditions where mid-side coding becomes inefficient—the decoder skips the \(M/S\) rotation matrix entirely for those specific bands and decodes the left and right coefficients directly.

Through this combination of inverse matrix transformations, angle-based energy distribution, and dynamic frequency-band coupling, the WebM Opus decoder accurately reconstructs the full spatial dimension and perceived width of the original stereo field.
