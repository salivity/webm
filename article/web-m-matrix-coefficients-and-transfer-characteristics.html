WebM Matrix Coefficients and Transfer Characteristics

The WebM container format uses color metadata definitions derived from the Matroska specification to describe how video pixel values map to standard color spaces. This article details the specific values and standards that can be signaled for Matrix Coefficients and Transfer Characteristics within a WebM video track, enabling accurate color and dynamic range reproduction for SDR and HDR content.

Color Signaling in WebM

In a WebM container, color metadata is stored under the Colour element within a video track entry. These parameters follow the ITU-T H.273 / ISO/IEC 23001-8 (Coding-independent code points) standards, aligning container-level metadata with the bitstream color signaling used in modern codecs such as VP9 and AV1.

Matrix Coefficients

The MatrixCoefficients element determines the mathematical transformation used to derive luma and chroma signals from the RGB color primaries.

The signaled integer values map to the following standards:

  • 0 – GBR / Identity: Identity matrix, typically used for full-range RGB or GBR color representations.
  • 1 – BT.709: ITU-R BT.709-6, the standard matrix used for high-definition (HD) video.
  • 2 – Unspecified: The matrix coefficients are not explicitly defined.
  • 3 – Reserved / US FCC: FCC Title 47 Code of Federal Regulations.
  • 4 – BT.470BG: ITU-R BT.470-6 System B, G (standard PAL).
  • 5 – SMPTE 170M: SMPTE 170M (standard NTSC / BT.601).
  • 6 – SMPTE 240M: SMPTE 240M, an early high-definition standard.
  • 7 – YCoCg: Non-constant luminance YCoCg color space.
  • 8 – BT.2020 NCL: ITU-R BT.2020 non-constant luminance system, widely used in Ultra HD (UHD) and HDR delivery.
  • 9 – BT.2020 CL: ITU-R BT.2020 constant luminance system.
  • 10 – SMPTE ST 2085: SMPTE ST 2085 (specifically designed for Y'D'zD'x color representation).
  • 11 – Chroma-derived NCL: Chromaticity-derived non-constant luminance system.
  • 12 – Chroma-derived CL: Chromaticity-derived constant luminance system.
  • 13 – ICtCp: ITU-R BT.2100-0 ICtCp color space, designed for High Dynamic Range and Wide Color Gamut processing.

Transfer Characteristics

The TransferCharacteristics element defines the Opto-Electronic Transfer Function (OETF) or Electro-Optical Transfer Function (EOTF), which describes how pixel values scale across luminance levels (gamma/dynamic range).

The signaled integer values map to the following standards:

  • 1 – BT.709: ITU-R BT.709-6 (standard gamma for SDR HD content).
  • 2 – Unspecified: The transfer characteristics are not defined.
  • 3 – Reserved: Historically ITU-R BT.470M / System M (Gamma ~2.2).
  • 4 – BT.470BG: ITU-R BT.470-6 System B, G (Gamma 2.8).
  • 5 – SMPTE 170M: SMPTE 170M / ITU-R BT.601.
  • 6 – SMPTE 240M: SMPTE 240M.
  • 7 – Linear: Linear transfer characteristics (\(V = L_c\)).
  • 8 – Logarithmic (100:1): Logarithmic transfer characteristics with a 100:1 dynamic range.
  • 9 – Logarithmic (316.2:1): Logarithmic transfer characteristics with a 100 * \(\sqrt{10}\) : 1 dynamic range.
  • 10 – IEC 61966-2-4: xvYCC extended-gamut standard.
  • 11 – BT.1361: ITU-R BT.1361-0 extended color gamut.
  • 12 – sRGB / IEC 61966-2-1: Standard sRGB and sYCC color space.
  • 13 – BT.2020 (10-bit): ITU-R BT.2020 for 10-bit systems.
  • 14 – BT.2020 (12-bit): ITU-R BT.2020 for 12-bit systems.
  • 15 – SMPTE ST 2084 (PQ): Perceptual Quantizer, the EOTF standard used for HDR10 and Dolby Vision.
  • 16 – SMPTE ST 428-1: D-Cinema distribution master transfer function.
  • 17 – ARIB STD-B67 (HLG): Hybrid Log-Gamma, the HDR standard defined in ITU-R BT.2100 for broadcast applications.
