Convert 60fps to Smooth 30fps WebM Video

Converting 60fps video down to 30fps in the WebM format requires more than simply discarding every other frame; it demands proper frame blending or motion estimation to avoid jitter, paired with efficient VP9 or AV1 encoding. This guide covers how motion blur affects framerate reduction, the software tools required, and the exact commands and settings to produce a smooth, lightweight 30fps WebM file.

Why Direct 60fps to 30fps Conversion Looks Choppy

Cameras recording at 60fps typically use a faster shutter speed (such as 1/120s) to keep individual frames sharp. When you simply drop 30 frames every second, the resulting video lacks the natural motion blur associated with native 30fps footage (which typically uses a 1/60s shutter speed). This causes high-motion scenes to appear strobed or juddery. To make a 30fps conversion look smooth, you must blend adjacent frames or synthesize artificial motion blur during the downsampling process.

Method 1: Using FFmpeg (Command-Line Solution)

FFmpeg is the most direct tool for converting framerates and outputting WebM files. It provides built-in filters to blend frames together smoothly before encoding to the VP9 codec.

1. Frame Blending via tblend

The fastest way to achieve smooth motion without heavy computational overhead is using the tblend filter to merge consecutive frames:

ffmpeg -i input_60fps.mp4 -filter:v "tblend=all_mode=average,framestep=2" -c:v libvpx-vp9 -crf 30 -b:v 0 -c:a libopus output_30fps.webm
  • tblend=all_mode=average: Blends every two adjacent frames together, creating simulated motion blur.
  • framestep=2: Drops the blended stream from 60fps to 30fps.
  • -c:v libvpx-vp9: Uses the modern, widely compatible VP9 WebM codec.
  • -crf 30 -b:v 0: Sets constant quality mode (lower values mean higher quality; 20–35 is standard).
  • -c:a libopus: Encodes audio into the high-efficiency Opus format.

2. Motion Interpolation via minterpolate

For complex motion where simple blending causes ghosting, motion interpolation analyzes pixel vectors to calculate smooth transitions:

ffmpeg -i input_60fps.mp4 -filter:v "minterpolate=fps=30:mi_mode=mci:mc_mode=aobmc:vsbmc=1" -c:v libvpx-vp9 -crf 30 -b:v 0 -c:a libopus output_smooth_30fps.webm

Note: minterpolate requires significantly more processing time than tblend.

Method 2: Using Video Editing Software (GUI Solution)

If you are using video editors like DaVinci Resolve, Adobe Premiere Pro, or HandBrake:

  1. DaVinci Resolve:

    • Import your 60fps footage into a 30fps timeline.
    • Go to the Inspector > Retime and Scaling.
    • Set Retime Process to Optical Flow and Motion Estimation to Speed Warp (or Enhanced Better).
    • In the Deliver tab, set the format to WebM and codec to VP9.

  2. Adobe Premiere Pro:

    • Place the 60fps clip into a 30fps sequence.
    • Right-click the clip, select Time Interpolation, and choose Optical Flow or Frame Blending.
    • Export via Adobe Media Encoder using the WebM/VP9 format.

  3. HandBrake:

    • Under the Video tab, set Framerate (FPS) to 30.
    • Select Peak Framerate or Constant Framerate.
    • Set Video Encoder to VP9 or AV1.
    • Note that HandBrake uses basic frame dropping by default; for simulated motion blur, FFmpeg or an NLE is preferred.

Recommended WebM Encoding Parameters

To ensure optimal playback and compatibility across modern browsers:

  • Container: WebM (.webm)
  • Video Codec: VP9 (libvpx-vp9) or AV1 (libsvtav1)
  • Audio Codec: Opus (libopus) at 96–128 kbps
  • Color Range: YUV420p for universal browser support (-pix_fmt yuv420p)
