Rate-Distortion Cost Math in WebM Motion Search

During the motion search phase of WebM codecs (principally VP8 and VP9 via libvpx), the encoder decides the optimal motion vector by evaluating candidate displacement vectors against a Rate-Distortion (RD) cost function. This process uses Lagrangian optimization to balance prediction fidelity against the bit budget needed to transmit motion data. The encoder minimizes a cost function \(J\) composed of an error metric (Distortion), an entropy estimate (Rate), and a Lagrange multiplier derived from the frame's quantization parameter.

The Lagrangian Cost Function

The primary formula executed during motion estimation is:

\[J(\mathbf{v}) = D(\mathbf{v}) + \lambda_{\text{motion}} \cdot R(\mathbf{v} - \mathbf{v}_{\text{pred}})\]

  • \(J(\mathbf{v})\) represents the total motion-search RD cost for a candidate motion vector \(\mathbf{v} = (v_x, v_y)\).
  • \(D(\mathbf{v})\) represents the distortion between the source block and the motion-compensated reference block.
  • \(R(\mathbf{v} - \mathbf{v}_{\text{pred}})\) represents the estimated number of bits required to encode the motion vector difference (MVD).
  • \(\lambda_{\text{motion}}\) represents the Lagrange multiplier that weights rate against distortion.

1. Distortion Metrics (\(D\))

Depending on the search stage—coarse integer-pixel search, fractional-pixel search, or final mode evaluation—WebM encoders use one of three primary distortion calculations.

Sum of Absolute Differences (SAD): Used in early-stage integer motion estimation for computational efficiency:

\[\text{SAD}(\mathbf{v}) = \sum_{y=0}^{N-1} \sum_{x=0}^{M-1} |S(x, y) - R(x + v_x, y + v_y)|\]

where \(S(x,y)\) is the current block sample, \(R\) is the reference frame buffer, and \(M \times N\) is the block dimension (e.g., \(16 \times 16\), \(8 \times 8\)).

Sum of Absolute Transformed Differences (SATD): Used during sub-pel refinement to better correlate with frequency-domain residual coding without computing a full discrete cosine transform:

\[\text{SATD}(\mathbf{v}) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{u, v} |H \cdot (S - R_{\mathbf{v}}) \cdot H^T|\]

where \(H\) is a normalized Walsh-Hadamard transform matrix applied to residual differences.

Sum of Squared Errors (SSE): Used in full rate-distortion optimization (RDO) stages:

\[\text{SSE}(\mathbf{v}) = \sum_{y=0}^{N-1} \sum_{x=0}^{M-1} (S(x, y) - R(x + v_x, y + v_y))^2\]

2. Motion Vector Rate Calculation (\(R\))

WebM codecs transmit motion vectors differentially relative to a predicted motion vector (\(\mathbf{v}_{\text{pred}}\)) generated from neighboring spatial or temporal blocks.

The motion vector difference (MVD) is calculated component-wise:

\[\Delta v_x = v_x - v_{\text{pred}, x}\] \[\Delta v_y = v_y - v_{\text{pred}, y}\]

The rate cost is the sum of bits required to encode both residual components through the codec's entropy engine (tree probabilities in VP8, or non-stationary context models in VP9):

\[R(\mathbf{v} - \mathbf{v}_{\text{pred}}) = \text{Cost}_{x}(\Delta v_x) + \text{Cost}_{y}(\Delta v_y)\]

In libvpx, these bit counts are pre-calculated into lookup tables based on the Shannon entropy of the syntax trees:

\[\text{Cost}(\Delta v) = -\sum_{i} \log_2(P(\text{bit}_i))\]

where \(P(\text{bit}_i)\) is the context-dependent probability of each decision bit traversing the tree to encode the sign, magnitude class, and fractional offsets of the vector delta.

3. The Lagrange Multiplier (\(\lambda\))

The trade-off factor \(\lambda_{\text{motion}}\) controls the stringency of the rate penalty. It is directly tied to the frame-level Quantization Parameter (\(Q\) or \(qindex \in [0, 255]\)).

For squared error distortion (SSE), the theoretical relationship between the mode-selection multiplier \(\lambda_{\text{mode}}\) and the quantization step size \(\Delta_Q\) is:

\[\lambda_{\text{mode}} = c \cdot \Delta_Q^2\]

Because fast motion estimation uses absolute difference metrics (SAD/SATD) rather than squared differences, the metric scale is linear rather than quadratic. Therefore, the motion search multiplier relates to \(\lambda_{\text{mode}}\) by the square root:

\[\lambda_{\text{motion}} \propto \sqrt{\lambda_{\text{mode}}} \propto \Delta_Q\]

In implementation, libvpx maps the active \(qindex\) to fixed-point integer lookup tables:

\[\lambda_{\text{motion}} = \text{vp9\_sad\_per\_bit\_lut}[qindex]\]

This ensures that at high quantization levels (low bitrates, large \(\Delta_Q\)), \(\lambda_{\text{motion}}\) heavily penalizes large motion vector deviations, favoring zero or near-predictor vectors. Conversely, at low quantization levels (high bitrates, small \(\Delta_Q\)), \(\lambda_{\text{motion}}\) decreases, allowing the search to select vectors that minimize residual distortion regardless of the bit overhead.
