Calculating Cache Hit Ratios for WebM Video Ladders

Video-on-demand (VOD) platforms calculate caching hit ratios for multi-bitrate WebM ladders by evaluating segment-level delivery metrics across distinct encoding profiles rather than treating a video title as a single entity. Because adaptive bitrate streaming divides WebM streams into hundreds of short chunks across varying resolutions and bitrates, Content Delivery Networks (CDNs) track both request-based and byte-weighted cache events at the edge. This article breaks down the mathematical formulations, data collection methods, and adaptive streaming dynamics platforms use to determine these metrics accurately.

The Structure of Multi-Bitrate WebM Streams

In modern VOD pipelines, multi-bitrate WebM ladders are deployed via Dynamic Adaptive Streaming over HTTP (DASH). The source video is encoded into a ladder of discrete renditions—ranging from low-resolution (e.g., 360p at 400 kbps) to high-resolution (e.g., 4K at 12 Mbps)—using codecs such as VP9 or AV1, alongside Opus audio.

Each rendition is partitioned into discrete chunk files (typically 2 to 6 seconds in duration) or indexed via byte ranges within a single initialization-segmented WebM container. Because the client player dynamically shifts between these profiles based on available network bandwidth, a single view of a video rarely pulls from only one rendition. Consequently, cache evaluation must operate at the chunk or byte level rather than the session level.

Request Hit Ratio vs. Byte Hit Ratio

CDNs and VOD platforms calculate two distinct cache hit ratios (CHR) to assess performance:

1. Request Cache Hit Ratio (R-CHR)

The Request Cache Hit Ratio measures the percentage of segment requests served directly from the CDN edge cache without querying the origin storage:

\[\text{R-CHR} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Cache Hits}}{\text{Total Cache Hits} + \text{Total Cache Misses}} \right) \times 100\]

While simple, R-CHR can produce misleading optimizations. For example, a 360p segment and a 4K segment both count as a single request, but the 4K segment consumes significantly more network resources on a cache miss.

2. Byte Cache Hit Ratio (B-CHR)

The Byte Cache Hit Ratio weights the calculation based on the payload size of each segment, providing an accurate reflection of bandwidth offload:

\[\text{B-CHR} = \left( \frac{\text{Bytes Served from Cache}}{\text{Total Bytes Served from Cache} + \text{Total Bytes Fetched from Origin}} \right) \times 100\]

In multi-bitrate WebM architectures, B-CHR is the primary financial and operational metric because egress costs and origin load scale directly with byte volume.

Per-Rendition Calculation and Aggregation

VOD edge servers log every transaction with metadata that includes the uniform resource identifier (URI), HTTP response status, cache state (e.g., HIT, MISS, REVALIDATED), bytes transferred, and upstream response time. Platforms extract ladder parameters directly from the request URI structure (e.g., /video_id/webm_1080p_4500k/segment_0042.webm).

To evaluate performance across the ladder, platforms aggregate metrics across two dimensions:

  1. Per-Rendition Hit Ratio: Evaluates the hit ratio for specific rungs of the ladder. Lower renditions often have higher hit ratios during the initial buffer phase (the startup segment), while middle renditions (e.g., 720p or 1080p) usually capture the majority of steady-state playback requests.
  2. Global Asset Hit Ratio: Aggregates all segments across all renditions for a specific piece of content:

\[\text{Global B-CHR} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \text{Cache Bytes Served}(R_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} \text{Total Bytes Delivered}(R_i)} \times 100\]

Where \(R_i\) represents each individual rendition in the WebM ladder, from \(1\) to \(n\).

Impact of ABR Dynamics on Cache Hit Rates

Calculating cache efficiency for multi-bitrate WebM requires platforms to account for three client-side behaviors:

  • Initial Playback Convergence: Most Adaptive Bitrate (ABR) algorithms request the lowest or a pre-configured mid-tier segment first to minimize Time to First Frame (TTFF). These initial segments generate disproportionately high hit ratios because nearly all viewers request the same introductory chunks.
  • Ladder Dispersion: When a video has too many rungs (e.g., 12 variants instead of 5 or 6), viewer requests fragment across slightly different bitrates. This fragmentation dilutes edge cache density, causing cache churn and driving down both R-CHR and B-CHR.
  • Audio Track Independence: WebM ladders typically decouple audio (Opus) from video renditions. Because audio is delivered as a single stream across all video renditions, its hit ratio is consistently higher than any individual video track, which platforms often isolate from video-only CHR calculations to avoid skewed data.
