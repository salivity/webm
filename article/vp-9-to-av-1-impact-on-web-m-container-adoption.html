VP9 to AV1: Impact on WebM Container Adoption

The transition from Google’s VP9 codec to the next-generation AV1 codec represents a major milestone in web video efficiency, but it directly alters the deployment and relevance of the WebM container format. While WebM served as the standard open-source vehicle for VP8 and VP9, AV1's widespread industry support has loosened the container's monopoly over open web video. This shift has led to increased container fragmentation, broader adoption of the MP4 container (ISOBMFF) for AV1 delivery, and a functional repositioning of WebM in modern web infrastructure.

The Historical Synergy Between VP9 and WebM

WebM was developed by Google as a royalty-free, lightweight alternative to proprietary formats like MP4, built on a profile of the Matroska (MKV) format. Throughout the lifecycle of VP8 and VP9, WebM was the primary, often mandatory, container for delivering these streams natively in web browsers and through major platforms like YouTube. As a result, VP9 adoption directly drove WebM adoption across video hosting providers, CDNs, and browser playback engines.

AV1 and Container Agnosticism

Developed by the Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia)—a consortium including Google, Apple, Microsoft, Netflix, and Amazon—AV1 was engineered with cross-industry consensus. Unlike VP9, AV1 was not designed to prioritize a single container format. The specification defined standardized bindings for both WebM (Matroska) and the ISO Base Media File Format (ISOBMFF / MP4).

Because major streaming platforms already rely heavily on ISOBMFF-based packaging for protocols like HLS and DASH, these platforms predominantly package AV1 streams inside MP4 or CMAF (Common Media Application Format) containers rather than WebM.

Key Impacts on WebM Adoption

  1. Reduced Dependency for Next-Gen Video: The transition to AV1 removes the technical necessity of using WebM to access cutting-edge open video compression. Engineering teams can leverage AV1 within existing MP4-centric delivery pipelines without modifying their container architecture.
  2. Standardization Around CMAF: As streaming platforms move toward low-latency CMAF to unify delivery across Apple and non-Apple ecosystems, WebM loses ground in high-volume adaptive bitrate (ABR) streaming workflows.
  3. Niche Consolidation for Real-Time and Web-Native Media: Despite losing share in general streaming pipelines, WebM retains high adoption in real-time communications via WebRTC, native browser media capture via the MediaRecorder API, and simple web-embedded video elements where lightweight packaging is preferred.

Conclusion

The transition from VP9 to AV1 breaks the tight coupling between open video codecs and the WebM format. While AV1 accelerates the adoption of open-source video standards globally, it simultaneously dilutes the market share of the WebM container, shifting the industry toward unified container standards like MP4 and CMAF for large-scale video delivery.
