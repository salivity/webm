Why Wikipedia Mandates Open Video Formats Like WebM

Wikipedia mandates open formats like WebM and Ogg Theora for all community video contributions to uphold its mission of free, unrestricted access to knowledge. By rejecting proprietary codecs such as H.264 or MP4, the Wikimedia Foundation ensures that multimedia content remains royalty-free, globally accessible, and usable across all platforms without legal risks or software licensing constraints. This policy aligns technological infrastructure with Wikipedia's philosophical commitment to open-source principles and long-term digital preservation.

Alignment with Open Knowledge Principles

Wikipedia is built on the premise that educational content should be freely accessible to read, modify, and redistribute. Using proprietary formats would create a barrier where the underlying technology contradicts the openness of the content itself. Open formats ensure that no single corporation owns or controls the way public knowledge is delivered and experienced.

Avoiding Patent Licensing and Royalties

Widely used video formats such as H.264 and H.265 are governed by patent pools (such as MPEG LA) that require licensing fees for certain types of distribution and software decoding. If Wikipedia adopted these codecs:

  • Downstream users redistributing Wikipedia content could face copyright or patent infringement claims.
  • Third-party developers building open-source tools to interact with Wikipedia would be burdened by licensing restrictions.
  • Wikimedia Foundation would be exposed to changing licensing terms and potential financial liabilities.

WebM, which uses royalty-free video codecs like VP8 and VP9, provides a legal guarantee that anyone can use the media files without paying royalties.

Ensuring Universal Software Compatibility

Proprietary codecs often rely on proprietary playback software or operating system licenses, creating hurdles for users on free and open-source platforms like Linux. Open formats are natively supported by modern web standards and open-source web browsers without requiring commercial third-party plugins. This allows users on any device or operating system to view and edit community video files seamlessly.

Digital Preservation and Independence

Proprietary codecs can be deprecated, altered, or locked behind paywalls at the discretion of their patent holders. Open-format specifications and reference source code are publicly documented and permanently available. Mandating formats like WebM ensures that multimedia records contributed by the community will remain readable and archivable for future generations without dependency on commercial vendors.
