Psychoacoustic Frequency Masking in WebM Vorbis

When encoding audio using the Vorbis codec in a WebM container, frequency masking thresholds are determined by an advanced psychoacoustic model based on human auditory perception. This model combines critical band rate scaling (the Bark scale), absolute threshold of hearing curves, tonality-dependent spectral masking, and energy spreading functions. By transforming audio signals into the frequency domain via the Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT), the encoder calculates the precise signal-to-mask ratio across frequency bands to eliminate inaudible data and optimize bitrate allocation without perceptible quality loss.

Critical Band Analysis and Bark Scale Mapping

The human ear processes sound through overlapping auditory filters known as critical bands. Vorbis maps linear MDCT spectral bins to a nonlinear psychoacoustic scale—typically the Bark scale or Equivalent Rectangular Bandwidth (ERB). This mapping reflects the cochlea's higher frequency resolution at low frequencies and wider bandwidth integration at high frequencies. Masking calculations are performed across these critical bands rather than on individual linear frequencies.

Absolute Threshold of Hearing (ATH)

The psychoacoustic model incorporates a baseline curve representing the Absolute Threshold of Hearing (ATH). The ATH defines the minimum sound pressure level required for a pure tone to be detected in a completely quiet environment. Any spectral components falling below this frequency-dependent curve are discarded immediately, serving as the lower boundary for the global masking threshold.

Tone-Masking-Noise and Noise-Masking-Tone

Vorbis differentiates between tonal (sinusoidal) components and noise-like spectral components to compute simultaneous (spectral) masking:

  • Tonality Estimation: The encoder evaluates spectral peaks and phase predictability across successive audio frames to assign a tonality index to different regions of the spectrum.
  • Masking Power Calculation: Tonal components have narrower, sharper masking curves, whereas noise-like components generate broader masking thresholds over surrounding frequencies. Noise-masking-noise and noise-masking-tone algorithms establish how much quantization noise can be concealed beneath dominant audio signals.

Spreading Functions

Masking energy does not remain confined to a single critical band; it extends to adjacent frequencies. Vorbis uses psychoacoustic spreading functions to model this behavior:

  • Upward Spread of Masking: Strong lower frequencies mask higher frequencies with a relatively gentle decay slope.
  • Downward Spread of Masking: Masking extends to lower frequencies with a much steeper attenuation curve. The convolution of the signal energy with these spreading functions creates the composite simultaneous masking curve across the entire spectrum.

Dynamic Floor Modeling (Floor 1)

Once the frequency masking thresholds are established, Vorbis synthesizes this information into a spectral envelope parameter known as the "Floor" (predominantly using the Floor 1 configuration). Floor 1 computes a piecewise linear approximation of the masking threshold in the decibel/Bark domain.

The floor serves two primary purposes:

  1. It functions as the master dynamic masking curve for that audio frame.
  2. It is subtracted from the raw MDCT spectrum to generate the "Residue," which contains the fine spectral details normalized against the masking curve.

Quantization and Noise Allocation

The residue is quantized using vector quantization codebooks. The psychoacoustic model ensures that quantization distortion is shaped to match the Floor curve, keeping the Noise-to-Mask Ratio (NMR) below the threshold of audibility. By concentrating quantization noise beneath louder frequencies and utilizing the calculated masking limits, Vorbis delivers transparent audio compression within WebM media streams.
