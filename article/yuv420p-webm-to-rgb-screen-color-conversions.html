YUV420p WebM to RGB Screen Color Conversions

Rendering a YUV420p WebM video file onto an RGB display requires a multi-stage transformation pipeline that converts subsampled, compressed color information into full-resolution pixel values matching the physical monitor. This process involves chroma upsampling from 4:2:0 to 4:4:4, scaling limited range values to full range, multiplying by a matrix defined by video standards (such as BT.601 or BT.709) to obtain R'G'B' components, and finally applying display-level color management and transfer functions to output accurate colors to the hardware screen.

1. Chroma Upsampling (4:2:0 to 4:4:4)

WebM files using the YUV420p pixel format store luminance (\(Y\)) at full resolution, while the two chrominance channels (\(U/C_b\) and \(V/C_r\)) are subsampled by half both horizontally and vertically. Before matrix conversion, the video decoder or GPU shader must reconstruct the missing chroma samples:

  • Spatial Interpolation: For every \(2 \times 2\) block of \(Y\) samples, there is only one \(U\) and one \(V\) sample. The renderer uses bilinear filtering, bicubic interpolation, or box filtering to upscale the chroma planes to match the luminance resolution.
  • Chroma Siting Adjustment: The interpolation respects the chroma sample location specified in the WebM container (e.g., VP8, VP9, or AV1 metadata), typically centered or left-aligned relative to the luma grid.

2. Quantization and Range Expansion

Most video content is encoded using limited (studio) range rather than full range.

  • Limited Range: \(Y\) values span from \(16\) to \(235\), while \(U\) and \(V\) span from \(16\) to \(240\) (in an 8-bit representation).
  • Expansion Formula: The limited-range values are normalized and expanded to a standard floating-point scale \([0.0, 1.0]\) or full integer range \([0, 255]\): \[Y_{\text{norm}} = \frac{Y - 16}{219}\] \[U_{\text{norm}} = \frac{U - 128}{224}\] \[V_{\text{norm}} = \frac{V - 128}{224}\] If the bitstream indicates full-range YUV, the offset normalization instead maps \([0, 255]\) directly.

3. YUV to Non-Linear RGB Matrix Conversion

Once aligned and scaled, the \(Y'U'V'\) components are transformed into non-linear, gamma-compressed \(R'G'B'\) using a \(3 \times 3\) color matrix determined by the bitstream metadata:

  • BT.709 (HD/Web Standard): The most common standard for WebM videos: \[R' = Y_{\text{norm}} + 1.5748 \cdot V_{\text{norm}}\] \[G' = Y_{\text{norm}} - 0.1873 \cdot U_{\text{norm}} - 0.4681 \cdot V_{\text{norm}}\] \[B' = Y_{\text{norm}} + 1.8556 \cdot U_{\text{norm}}\]
  • BT.601 (SD Standard): Applied for standard-definition resolutions if explicitly flagged.
  • BT.2020: Used for HDR content in newer VP9 or AV1 WebM streams.

4. Electro-Optical Transfer Function (EOTF) and Linearization

To ensure proper blending, compositing, and color accuracy across modern rendering pipelines, the non-linear \(R'G'B'\) values may be linearized:

  • Decoding Gamma: The renderer applies the inverse transfer function (such as the BT.709 or sRGB transfer curve) to transform \(R'G'B'\) into linear light \(RGB\).
  • Pass-Through Approximation: In legacy or lightweight pipelines, this step is omitted, and the non-linear \(R'G'B'\) values are fed directly to the framebuffer under the assumption that the source gamma matches standard sRGB/display gamma.

5. Color Gamut Mapping and Display Output

The final step aligns the decoded color space with the physical display's capabilities:

  • Gamut Conversion: If the video's color primaries (e.g., BT.709) differ from the display's native gamut (e.g., Display P3 or Adobe RGB), a matrix transforms the linear values into the target display space.
  • Display OETF/Gamma Application: The GPU hardware or OS compositor applies the monitor's calibrated Electro-Optical Transfer Function (typically a ~2.2 gamma or sRGB curve) to output the final 8-bit or 10-bit digital RGB signal sent via HDMI, DisplayPort, or internal panel interface.
