VP9 Lossless Mode: Bypassing Transform and Quantization

WebM achieves lossless video compression through the VP9 codec by disabling the traditional lossy stages of the video pipeline, specifically the frequency transform and quantization processes. In standard video encoding, these two stages discard imperceptible high-frequency visual data to reduce file sizes. By configuring VP9 to run in lossless mode, the encoder bypasses these operations entirely, passing raw prediction residuals directly to the arithmetic entropy coder to ensure an exact, bit-for-bit recreation of the source pixels.

In a conventional VP9 lossy encoding pipeline, the encoder first predicts pixel values using intra-frame (spatial) or inter-frame (temporal) prediction. It subtracts this prediction from the actual source image to produce a block of residual differences. In lossy mode, this residual block undergoes a mathematical transform—either a Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) or an Asymmetric Discrete Sine Transform (ADST)—which converts spatial pixel variations into frequency coefficients. Next, quantization scales down and rounds these coefficients by dividing them by a quantization parameter (QP), which irreversibly discards precision to save bandwidth.

When lossless mode is enabled in VP9 (typically triggered by setting the quantizer parameter to zero), the pipeline alters this execution path. Prediction still takes place because predicting neighboring pixels or motion vectors remains a powerful, mathematically reversible method for reducing spatial redundancy. However, once the residual values are calculated, the transform stage is skipped. The encoder does not compute DCT or ADST operations on the pixel blocks. Skipping the transform eliminates the floating-point and rounding discrepancies inherent in frequency space conversion and integer approximation.

Following the transform bypass, the quantization stage is also completely circumvented. Because there are no frequency coefficients to scale and round, the raw residual values retain their exact mathematical integer values. Instead of mapping values into coarser buckets, the codec treats the quantizer scale factor as zero, meaning no division or rounding logic is executed.

The unaltered residual integers are then routed directly to VP9's adaptive binary arithmetic coder (the entropy engine). The entropy coder compresses the spatial prediction modes, motion vectors, and raw residual numbers into the final WebM bitstream using lossless probabilistic modeling. Because arithmetic coding is inherently reversible, the decoder simply unpacks the bitstream, runs the identical prediction algorithm, and adds the raw residuals back to the prediction block, yielding pixel data identical to the original uncompressed source.
