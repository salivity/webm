How WebM Handles Non-Square Pixel Aspect Ratios

This article explains how the WebM media container format handles non-square pixel aspect ratios (PAR) during rendering. Built on a subset of the Matroska (MKV) container profile, WebM separates the encoded pixel dimensions from the final display dimensions using metadata tags. By reading these container-level parameters, video players and web browsers can stretch or compress non-square pixels to display anamorphic video correctly without altering the underlying encoded stream.

Storage Resolution vs. Display Resolution

In video encoding, non-square pixels—often referred to as Sample Aspect Ratio (SAR) or Pixel Aspect Ratio (PAR)—occur when the shape of an individual pixel is rectangular rather than square. Common examples include anamorphic widescreen formats and digitized standard-definition media (such as PAL or NTSC).

WebM manages this distinction by declaring two separate sets of video dimensions within its EBML (Extensible Binary Meta Language) track headers:

  • PixelWidth and PixelHeight: Define the raw storage resolution (the actual number of coded pixels in the video stream, such as 720x480).
  • DisplayWidth and DisplayHeight: Define the target rendering resolution (the intended dimensions when presented to the viewer, such as 853x480 for a 16:9 aspect ratio).

The Rendering Process

When a media player or browser engine (such as Chromium or Gecko) loads a WebM file, the rendering pipeline follows these steps to resolve non-square pixels:

  1. Header Parsing: The demuxer reads the TrackEntry element within the WebM container, extracting PixelWidth, PixelHeight, DisplayWidth, and DisplayHeight.
  2. Decoding: The video decoder (handling VP8, VP9, or AV1) decodes the frames at their native storage resolution.
  3. Aspect Ratio Calculation: The player calculates the intended display aspect ratio (DAR) using the formula: \[\text{DAR} = \frac{\text{DisplayWidth}}{\text{DisplayHeight}}\] The resulting Pixel Aspect Ratio (PAR) is derived by comparing the display aspect ratio against the storage aspect ratio: \[\text{PAR} = \left(\frac{\text{DisplayWidth}}{\text{PixelWidth}}\right) \div \left(\frac{\text{DisplayHeight}}{\text{PixelHeight}}\right)\]
  4. Hardware Scaling and Composition: Rather than re-sampling the raw video data on the CPU, the rendering engine passes the decoded frame to the GPU alongside a transformation matrix. The GPU scales the texture non-uniformly to match the calculated display aspect ratio before presenting it on the screen.

Container vs. Bitstream Metadata

WebM video codecs (VP8, VP9, and AV1) can also store aspect ratio metadata directly inside their sequence headers. However, WebM specification standards dictate that container-level metadata (DisplayWidth and DisplayHeight) takes precedence or must match the bitstream headers.

Most modern rendering engines rely primarily on the container-level values to allocate the layout space (such as the HTML5 <video> element's bounding box) before decoding begins, ensuring seamless playback without layout shifts.
