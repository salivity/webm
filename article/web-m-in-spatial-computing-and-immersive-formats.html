WebM in Spatial Computing and Immersive Formats

WebM remains a foundational open-source media container for the browser-based internet, yet it faces unique challenges and opportunities in the transition toward immersive spatial computing. While proprietary ecosystems have rallied around MV-HEVC for stereoscopic depth and spatial video, WebM leverages codecs like VP9 and AV1 to dominate open, royalty-free WebXR streaming and 360-degree interactive media. This article examines WebM’s technical capabilities, structural limitations, and future role alongside emerging spatial computing standards.

The Role of WebM in Immersive WebXR

WebM, developed by Google and maintained as an open standard, is the primary delivery mechanism for video within WebXR experiences. Because WebM natively supports VP8, VP9, and AV1 video streams, it delivers high compression efficiency without licensing fees. In browser-based virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR), WebM files are widely used to project standard 2D, 180-degree, and 360-degree equirectangular video onto virtual geometry inside headsets like Meta Quest and Apple Vision Pro.

The Rise of MV-HEVC and WebM's Limitations

Spatial computing requires video formats that store separate stereo views (left and right eye) efficiently, alongside spatial audio and camera metadata. Apple popularized MV-HEVC (Multiview High Efficiency Video Coding), packaged in QuickTime/MP4 containers, which encodes primary 2D video while storing secondary eye data as a lightweight delta layer.

WebM does not currently feature a standardized, widely adopted multiview layer comparable to MV-HEVC. Traditional stereoscopic WebM files rely on side-by-side (SBS) or over-under (OU) frame packing. While functional, frame-packed video halves spatial resolution per eye or doubles the decoded pixel dimensions, making it less bandwidth-efficient and harder to hardware-decode smoothly compared to native multiview standards.

AV1 Integration and Future Standardization

WebM's future in spatial computing is tied to the development of AV1 extensions by the Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia). Ongoing work into multiview extensions for AV1 aims to provide an open-source equivalent to MV-HEVC. Once multiview AV1 reaches widespread implementation, WebM containers will be capable of carrying layered 3D stereoscopic tracks, parallax data, and spatial audio metadata directly within open web standards.

Market Positioning Summary

WebM is positioned as the accessible, open-web standard for spatial streaming rather than a high-end authoring format for standalone spatial operating systems. While native applications on platforms like visionOS prioritize MV-HEVC for hardware-accelerated, deep-depth playback, WebM remains essential for cross-platform, royalty-free immersive web experiences where frictionless browser access is critical.
