WebM Video Quantization Step Size Formulas

WebM video compression, powered primarily by the VP8 and VP9 codecs, relies on non-linear quantization mechanics to regulate bitrate and visual fidelity. This article details the mathematical formulas, scaling functions, and index mappings that determine quantization step sizes across WebM quality levels.

Fundamentals of WebM Quantization

In transform-based video encoding, quantization reduces the precision of Discrete Cosine Transform (DCT) or Asymmetric Discrete Sine Transform (ADST) coefficients. The forward quantization formula transforms a frequency coefficient \(C\) into a quantized level \(C_q\):

\[C_q = \text{sgn}(C) \cdot \left\lfloor \frac{|C| + f}{Q_{\text{step}}} \right\rfloor\]

Where:

  • \(C\) is the transformed transform coefficient.
  • \(Q_{\text{step}}\) is the actual quantization step size.
  • \(f\) is a rounding offset (typically between \(0\) and \(Q_{\text{step}} / 2\)).
  • \(\text{sgn}(C)\) preserves the sign of the input.

During decoding, dequantization reconstructs the approximate coefficient value \(\hat{C}\):

\[\hat{C} = C_q \cdot Q_{\text{step}}\]

The value of \(Q_{\text{step}}\) directly dictates lossiness: larger values discard more high-frequency detail for lower bitrates, while smaller values preserve fidelity.

VP8 Quantization Step Size Formulas

VP8 maps a baseline Quantization Index (\(q \in [0, 63]\)) to step sizes using discrete lookup formulas for DC and AC components across luma (Y) and chroma (U, V) planes.

While implemented in standard libraries via the vp8_ac_qlookup and vp8_dc_qlookup tables, these step sizes follow piecewise exponential curves governed by:

1. Luma AC Step Size (\(Q_{\text{step, AC}}\))

\[Q_{\text{step, AC}}(q) \approx \begin{cases} 4 \cdot q + 4 & \text{for } 0 \le q \le 3 \\ \left\lfloor 0.0416 \cdot q^2 + 1.343 \cdot q + 14.1 \right\rfloor & \text{for } 4 \le q \le 63 \end{cases}\]

2. Luma DC Step Size (\(Q_{\text{step, DC}}\))

Low-frequency DC coefficients use finer resolution to prevent blocking artifacts in flat regions:

\[Q_{\text{step, DC}}(q) = \min\left(Q_{\text{step, AC}}(q), \left\lfloor 1.32 \cdot q + 4 \right\rfloor\right)\]

VP9 Quantization Step Size Formulas

VP9 expands the quantizer index range to \(Q_{\text{index}} \in [0, 255]\), providing significantly finer granularity. The continuous mathematical model that generates the VP9 step size lookup tables approximates an exponential scaling curve:

\[Q_{\text{step}}(Q_{\text{index}}) \approx Q_{\text{min}} \cdot 2^{\frac{Q_{\text{index}}}{K}}\]

Where:

  • \(Q_{\text{min}}\) is the baseline minimum step size at \(Q_{\text{index}} = 0\).
  • \(K\) is the index interval over which the step size doubles (typically \(K \approx 32\) to \(40\) depending on bit depth).

Exact Implementation Formulation

The reference VP9 codec (libvpx) computes the exact 8-bit AC quantizer table using continuous piecewise polynomial and exponential fits:

\[Q_{\text{step, AC}}(q) = \begin{cases} q + 4 & \text{for } 0 \le q \le 4 \\ \left\lfloor 4.2 \cdot e^{0.0245 \cdot q} \right\rfloor & \text{for } 5 \le q \le 255 \end{cases}\]

For higher bit-depth streams (\(10\)-bit and \(12\)-bit), the base step size scales proportionally with the bit depth \(B\):

\[Q_{\text{step, B-bit}}(q) = Q_{\text{step, 8-bit}}(q) \cdot 2^{B - 8}\]

Delta Quantization Offsets

WebM codecs apply contextual offsets (\(\Delta Q\)) to alter the step size per frame, segment, or transform block. The final effective index \(Q_{\text{effective}}\) is computed prior to table conversion:

\[Q_{\text{effective}} = \text{clamp}\left(Q_{\text{base}} + \Delta Q_{\text{plane}} + \Delta Q_{\text{coeff}} + \Delta Q_{\text{segment}},\, 0,\, 255\right)\]

Where:

  • \(Q_{\text{base}}\) is the frame-level quality target.
  • \(\Delta Q_{\text{plane}}\) represents adjustments for Chroma channels (\(\text{UV}_{\text{dc\_delta}}\), \(\text{UV}_{\text{ac\_delta}}\)).
  • \(\Delta Q_{\text{coeff}}\) modulates high-frequency AC coefficients relative to DC coefficients (\(\text{Y}_{\text{dc\_delta}}\)).
  • \(\Delta Q_{\text{segment}}\) dynamically applies local step sizes for Region of Interest (ROI) and background segmentation.

CRF and Bitrate Mapping

In WebM encoding tools such as FFmpeg, the Constant Rate Factor (-crf) directly drives the selection of \(Q_{\text{base}}\). For VP9, the mapping function from user-defined CRF to the encoder's internal quantization index is a linear scale:

\[Q_{\text{base}} = \left\lfloor \frac{255}{63} \cdot \text{CRF} \right\rfloor \approx 4.0476 \cdot \text{CRF}\]

This mapping ensures that incremental increases in CRF produce uniform, exponential decreases in bitrate corresponding to the underlying step size formulas.
