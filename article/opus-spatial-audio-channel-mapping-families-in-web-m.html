Opus Spatial Audio Channel Mapping Families in WebM

This article provides an overview of the channel mapping families used to deliver spatial audio using the Opus audio codec within WebM containers. Spatial audio in WebM relies on standardized channel mapping families defined in the IETF Opus specification and Google’s spatial audio guidelines. Specifically, Channel Mapping Family 2 and Channel Mapping Family 3 are the designated families for encoding and rendering spatial and ambisonic audio content.

Channel Mapping Family 2: Pure Ambisonics

Channel Mapping Family 2 is dedicated to full-spherical spatial audio without head-locked stereo elements. It encodes Higher Order Ambisonics (HOA) soundfields.

  • Channel Ordering and Normalization: It uses Ambisonic Channel Number (ACN) component ordering and Schmidt Semi-Normalized (SN3D) normalization.
  • Channel Counts: The total number of channels must equal \((1 + N)^2\), where \(N\) represents the ambisonic order:
    • First-Order Ambisonics (FOA): 4 channels (\(N = 1\))
    • Second-Order Ambisonics (SOA): 9 channels (\(N = 2\))
    • Third-Order Ambisonics (TOA): 16 channels (\(N = 3\))
  • Use Case: Immersive environments, 360-degree video, and VR applications where the entire soundfield rotates dynamically with user head tracking.

Channel Mapping Family 3: Ambisonics with Non-Diegetic Stereo

Channel Mapping Family 3 extends Family 2 by adding a head-locked, non-diegetic stereo audio track to the ambisonic soundfield.

  • Channel Structure: The soundfield uses \((1 + N)^2 + 2\) channels. The first \((1 + N)^2\) channels carry the ACN/SN3D ambisonic components, while the final 2 channels carry standard left and right non-diegetic stereo audio.
    • First-Order + Stereo: 6 channels (\(4 + 2\))
    • Second-Order + Stereo: 11 channels (\(9 + 2\))
    • Third-Order + Stereo: 18 channels (\(16 + 2\))
  • Use Case: Interactive media, games, and VR videos that require spatialized environmental audio alongside static background music, voiceover narration, or UI sounds that should not rotate when the listener turns their head.

WebM Container Implementation

In a WebM container (identified with the Matroska Codec ID A_OPUS), the Opus header (OpusHead) specifies the spatial audio configuration via the following fields:

  1. Channel Count: Total number of encoded channels (e.g., 4 for 1st-order Family 2, 6 for 1st-order Family 3).
  2. Channel Mapping Family: Set to 2 or 3.
  3. Stream Count (\(M\)): Total number of distinct Opus streams multiplexed inside the payload.
  4. Coupled Stream Count (\(C\)): Number of two-channel (stereo-coupled) streams.
  5. Channel Mapping Table: An array mapping each output channel to an individual stream and channel index.

Non-Spatial Reference Families

For comparison, conventional channel mapping families in Opus do not support spherical spatialization:

  • Family 0: 1 or 2 channels (Mono or Stereo).
  • Family 1: 1 to 8 channels mapped to traditional surround speaker layouts (such as 5.1 or 7.1) using Vorbis channel ordering.
  • Family 255: Up to 255 discrete, unmapped channels without defined speaker geometry.
