Future of WebM Hardware Decoding in Mobile Chips

This article examines whether mobile System-on-Chip (SoC) manufacturers will continue prioritizing dedicated silicon blocks for the WebM container and its associated codecs (VP8, VP9, and AV1). As streaming demands shift and silicon die area becomes increasingly competitive, hardware manufacturers are moving away from codec-specific dedicated blocks in favor of flexible, multi-format Video Processing Units (VPUs) that emphasize next-generation standards like AV1.

The Role of WebM in Mobile Hardware

WebM, developed by Google, primarily houses VP8, VP9, and AV1 video streams alongside Vorbis or Opus audio. Historically, mobile hardware manufacturers like Qualcomm, MediaTek, Samsung, and Apple integrated dedicated silicon logic specifically to decode VP8 and VP9. This integration was critical because software-based decoding of high-resolution video quickly drains battery life and generates significant heat on mobile devices. Because platforms like YouTube relied heavily on VP9, hardware acceleration became a standard requirement for mobile chipsets.

Evolution Toward Unified Video Processing Units

Mobile hardware designers do not typically design standalone blocks strictly for a single container format like WebM. Instead, modern mobile SoCs integrate unified VPUs capable of decoding multiple formats—such as H.264, HEVC (H.265), VP9, and AV1—using shared fixed-function hardware pipelines.

While legacy WebM codecs (VP8 and VP9) are still supported in these VPUs to ensure backward compatibility, they are no longer the primary focus of silicon innovation. Chipmakers now allocate minimal die space to maintain legacy VP9 pipelines, often consolidating these operations into broader, generalized hardware execution units.

The Shift Toward AV1

The primary focus for royalty-free video hardware acceleration has transitioned to AV1, developed by the Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia). Modern flagship SoCs, including recent generations of Qualcomm Snapdragon, MediaTek Dimensity, and Apple A-series/M-series chips, now feature dedicated hardware AV1 decoders. Because AV1 can be encapsulated within the WebM container, mobile silicon continues to support the modern evolution of the WebM ecosystem, even as standalone priority for older VP8/VP9 blocks declines.

Silicon Real Estate and Power Optimization

Dedicated hardware logic offers the highest power efficiency for video decoding, which is essential for mobile battery life during continuous streaming. However, silicon die area is expensive. Hardware manufacturers must balance dedicated video silicon against the growing demand for Neural Processing Units (NPUs), upgraded GPU cores, and larger system-level caches. Consequently, chipmakers will continue to support the WebM ecosystem primarily through unified VPU designs that prioritize AV1, maintaining legacy WebM compatibility without dedicating bespoke hardware space exclusively to older formats.
