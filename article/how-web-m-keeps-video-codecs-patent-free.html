How WebM Keeps Video Codecs Patent-Free

The WebM Project ensures its video codecs, such as VP8 and VP9, remain strictly patent-unencumbered through a combination of rigorous prior art research, non-infringing engineering, broad defensive patent licensing, and formal legal settlements. By preemptively identifying proprietary technologies and enforcing a legally binding open-source ecosystem, the project protects developers and users from royalty claims and third-party patent litigation.

Intentional Clean-Room Design and Prior Art Analysis

During the development of WebM codecs, engineers actively audit algorithmic proposals against existing patent databases. If a specific compression technique is covered by an active patent, developers either engineer around the patent using alternative mathematical approaches or deliberately implement techniques established in prior art where patents have already expired. This proactive vetting minimizes the risk of inadvertently integrating proprietary intellectual property into the standard.

Royalty-Free Contributor License Agreements

Anyone contributing to the WebM Project must sign a Contributor License Agreement (CLA). Under this agreement, contributors grant a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, no-charge, royalty-free, irrevocable patent license for their contributions. This legal framework prevents any contributing individual or corporation from later asserting patent infringement claims against users, distributors, or implementers of the codec.

Defensive Patent Termination Clauses

WebM’s patent license includes a defensive termination clause designed to deter patent aggression. If a licensee files a patent infringement lawsuit against any entity, alleging that the WebM implementation infringes upon their patents, the patent rights granted to that litigating party under the WebM license are automatically terminated. This mutual deterrence mechanism discourages patent holders from targeting the WebM ecosystem.

Strategic Patent Acquisition and Industry Settlements

To resolve external patent challenges, Google, the steward of the WebM Project, has historically negotiated agreements with competing patent pools. In 2013, Google entered into a licensing agreement with MPEG LA and eleven individual patent holders. Under this agreement, Google acquired licenses for patents that were asserted to be essential to VP8, granting sublicensing rights to all VP8 users worldwide without royalties. This settlement effectively neutralized claims from one of the industry's largest patent consortia.

The AOMedia Patent Pool Partnership

WebM’s strategy expanded with the formation of the Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia), which developed AV1 as a successor to VP9. AOMedia brings together major technology companies—including Google, Microsoft, Apple, Amazon, and Mozilla—to pool their intellectual property. Members agree to cross-license their patents royalty-free and commit resources to a collective defense fund. This united legal front provides significant legal and financial backing, ensuring that open-source codec implementations remain viable and protected against external royalty demands.
