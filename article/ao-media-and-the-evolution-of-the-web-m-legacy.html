AOMedia and the Evolution of the WebM Legacy

The Alliance for Open Media (AOMedia) played a transformative role in advancing the legacy of the WebM project by uniting industry leaders to build a universally supported, royalty-free video standard. While Google initially introduced WebM to establish an open-source alternative to patented codecs, AOMedia expanded this vision by merging WebM's next-generation technology with other open video projects to create AV1. This transition shifted the royalty-free movement from a single-company initiative to a broad industry consensus, ensuring the long-term viability and mainstream adoption of open web media.

The Foundation Set by WebM

Google launched the WebM project in 2010 to provide an efficient, open, and royalty-free media file format for the web. WebM relied primarily on the VP8 and VP9 video codecs. While WebM achieved widespread support in web browsers and platforms like YouTube, it faced significant resistance from hardware manufacturers and other tech giants who remained committed to standard licensing pools such as MPEG's H.264 and HEVC.

Unification of Competing Open Codecs

By 2015, Google was actively developing VP10 as the direct successor to VP9 under the WebM umbrella. Simultaneously, other organizations were developing their own royalty-free formats, such as Cisco’s Thor and Mozilla and Xiph.Org’s Daala. Recognizing that a fragmented landscape would prevent any single open codec from defeating proprietary standards, Google, Mozilla, Cisco, Microsoft, Intel, Amazon, and Netflix founded the Alliance for Open Media.

Instead of releasing VP10 independently under the WebM brand, Google contributed its core VP10 codebase to AOMedia. The alliance merged the best elements of VP10, Daala, and Thor to create a single, unified open-source video codec: AV1 (AOMedia Video 1).

Securing Hardware and Industry Adoption

WebM's biggest hurdle was securing dedicated hardware decoding across consumer devices, as many chipmakers hesitated to support a format backed primarily by a single company. AOMedia solved this issue by bringing major semiconductor manufacturers—including Intel, AMD, ARM, Broadcom, and Nvidia—directly into the governing body. This collaboration ensured that AV1 received native hardware acceleration, fulfilling the original performance and efficiency goals of the WebM project at a global scale.

Expanding the Open Media Ecosystem

AOMedia did not discard WebM; rather, it institutionalized WebM's founding philosophy into a legally robust, patent-resilient framework. AOMedia established clear cross-licensing patent agreements among its members, shielding AV1 adopters from royalty disputes and patent uncertainty.

Through AOMedia, the groundwork laid by the WebM project evolved from an alternative web experiment into the foundation for the next generation of global internet video delivery.
