WebM Interlaced Video Field Order Metadata

This article provides an overview of the specific container-level metadata elements used to define interlacing and field order in the WebM format. Because WebM is derived from the Matroska (MKV) specification, it relies on standard EBML (Extensible Binary Meta Language) elements within its video track headers to signal how interlaced video frames should be decoded and rendered.

The Container Elements for Interlaced Video

Inside a WebM container, video track settings are encapsulated within the TrackEntry element under the Video sub-element. To define interlaced video properties, WebM utilizes two primary container-level metadata elements:

  1. FieldOrder (EBML ID: 0x9D): This is the specific element that defines the field order for interlaced video. It explicitly tells the demuxer and video renderer which field should be displayed first.
  2. FlagInterlaced (EBML ID: 0x9A): This element declares whether the video stream is interlaced, progressive, or undetermined.

Values for the FieldOrder Element

The FieldOrder element uses specific integer values to convey temporal and spatial layout:

  • 0 (Progressive): The video is progressive and has no interlaced fields.
  • 1 (Top Field First / TFF): The top field is captured and displayed before the bottom field.
  • 2 (Undetermined): The video is interlaced, but the field order is not known.
  • 6 (Bottom Field First / BFF): The bottom field is captured and displayed before the top field.
  • 9 (Top Field First with Swapped Fields): Interlaced frames with alternating fields where the top field is displayed first, but fields are stored in reverse spatial order.
  • 14 (Bottom Field First with Swapped Fields): Interlaced frames with alternating fields where the bottom field is displayed first, but fields are stored in reverse spatial order.

Values for the FlagInterlaced Element

Before reading the specific field order, parsers typically check the FlagInterlaced element:

  • 0: Undetermined
  • 1: Interlaced
  • 2: Progressive

Why Container-Level Metadata Matters

Specifying FieldOrder at the container level allows media players, transcoders, and deinterlacing filters to determine how to process the video stream without having to parse individual codec-level bitstream headers. This ensures proper field blending, line doubling, or motion-adaptive deinterlacing during playback.
