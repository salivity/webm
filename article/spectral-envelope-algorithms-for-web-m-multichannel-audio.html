Spectral Envelope Algorithms for WebM Multichannel Audio

The WebM container primarily encapsulates multi-channel audio using two lossy compression formats: Opus and Vorbis. Calculating the spectral envelope—the smoothed curve tracking the distribution of energy across the frequency spectrum—is essential for psychoacoustic bit allocation, spectral shaping, and multichannel spatial coding. This article outlines the primary mathematical and digital signal processing algorithms used to compute the spectral envelope in multi-channel WebM audio tracks, focusing on linear prediction, critical band energy analysis, and multichannel inter-channel coupling mechanisms.

Linear Predictive Coding (LPC)

Linear Predictive Coding represents the spectral envelope by modeling the audio signal as the output of an all-pole linear filter driven by an excitation signal. In WebM's Opus codec (specifically its speech-centric SILK layer) and Vorbis (via its legacy "Floor 0" envelope mode), LPC is a foundational envelope estimator.

  • Autocorrelation and Levinson-Durbin Recursion: The short-term spectral envelope is computed by windowing the time-domain signal, generating autocorrelation coefficients, and applying the Levinson-Durbin algorithm to compute optimal filter coefficients.
  • Line Spectral Frequencies (LSF / LSP): To preserve filter stability and facilitate multi-channel quantization, the LPC polynomial roots are converted into Line Spectral Pairs. In multi-channel setups, LSFs capture individual channel resonance distributions while minimizing cross-channel distortion during transmission.

Critical Band Energy Integration

The primary spectral envelope representation in both modern Vorbis and the CELT layer of Opus relies on dividing the frequency spectrum into psychoacoustically motivated subbands (approximating the Bark or ERB scales) and measuring energy within each band.

  • MDCT Subband Energy Calculation: The signal is transformed via the Modified Discrete Cosine Transform (MDCT). The spectrum is partitioned into non-linear critical bands. The spectral envelope is calculated by taking the root mean square (RMS) or logarithmic energy of the MDCT coefficients within each band: \[\text{Energy}_b = \log_2 \left( \sum_{k \in \text{Band}_b} X[k]^2 \right)\]
  • Piecewise Linear Interpolation (Vorbis Floor 1): Vorbis's standard "Floor 1" envelope algorithm models the spectral envelope as a series of connected linear segments in the log-frequency/log-amplitude domain. The envelope is defined by a sparse set of control points determined through non-linear curve fitting across critical bands, producing an efficient, low-artifact approximation of the spectrum.

Cepstral Smoothing and the True Envelope Algorithm

When analyzing arbitrary multi-channel audio extracted from WebM files (such as in acoustic analysis or speech enhancement), cepstral techniques are often utilized rather than direct codec-internal tools.

  • Homomorphic Deconvolution (Real Cepstrum): The Fourier transform of the signal is computed, followed by the logarithm of its magnitude, and then the inverse Fourier transform (IFFT) to yield the real cepstrum. Liftering (low-pass filtering in the quefrency domain) removes the excitation harmonics and isolates the slow-varying spectral envelope.
  • True Envelope Algorithm: An iterative cepstral smoothing algorithm that updates the cepstral coefficients until the resulting envelope completely encloses the spectral peaks rather than cutting through their averages. This is particularly valuable for complex multi-channel arrangements where phase cancellation between channels can distort harmonic peak representation.

Multi-Channel Processing and Channel Coupling Algorithms

In multi-channel configurations (such as 5.1 or 7.1 surround sound), calculating the spectral envelope for each channel independently leads to redundancy and inter-channel masking artifacts. Codecs inside WebM employ joint envelope algorithms:

  • Mid/Side (M/S) Matrixing: Envelopes are calculated on sum (\(M = (L+R)/\sqrt{2}\)) and difference (\(S = (L-R)/\sqrt{2}\)) channels. The spectral envelope of the side channel is evaluated relative to the mid channel to eliminate cross-talk and phase discrepancies.
  • Intensity Stereo and Dynamic Channel Coupling: At higher frequencies, human spatial localization depends primarily on energy envelopes rather than phase. The codec computes a single, shared composite spectral envelope for multiple surround channels, alongside a vector of scale factors (Inter-channel Level Differences, or ILDs) to reconstruct the spatial envelope profile for each output speaker.
  • Band-by-Band Energy Normalization: In Opus CELT, once the band energy envelope is computed across all channels, individual MDCT coefficients are normalized by their band energy. Multi-channel correlation matrices are then used to steer the energy vectors, allowing the decoder to restore the multi-channel envelope with precise spatial positioning.
