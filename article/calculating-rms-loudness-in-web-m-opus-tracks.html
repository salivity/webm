Calculating RMS Loudness in WebM Opus Tracks

This article outlines the mathematical process used to calculate Root-Mean-Square (RMS) loudness levels from audio stored within WebM Opus files. Because Opus is a lossy, transform-based audio format, calculating RMS loudness requires demuxing the WebM container, decoding Opus frames into linear Pulse Code Modulation (PCM) samples, and executing standard statistical power equations over a selected time window. The resulting values are then converted from a linear scale into decibels relative to full scale (dBFS) to reflect perceptual signal power.

1. Decoding to Linear PCM

Opus compresses audio using modified discrete cosine transforms (MDCT) and Linear Predictive Coding (SILK). Because RMS cannot be calculated directly from compressed frequency-domain data, the audio packets must first be extracted from the WebM container (a subset of Matroska) and decoded into uncompressed, discrete-time PCM samples.

Each decoded audio sample at discrete time index \(n\) is represented as a normalized floating-point amplitude value:

\[x[n] \in [-1.0, 1.0]\]

2. Defining the Analysis Window

RMS loudness is measured across a finite time window containing \(N\) samples. If the sampling rate is \(f_s\) (Opus typically decodes internally at 48,000 Hz) and the target measurement duration is \(T\) seconds, the window size \(N\) is calculated as:

\[N = f_s \times T\]

For an instantaneous or momentary window of 400 milliseconds at 48 kHz:

\[N = 48000 \times 0.4 = 19200 \text{ samples}\]

3. Squaring the Samples (Instantaneous Energy)

To eliminate negative values and calculate signal energy, each individual sample \(x[n]\) within the window is squared:

\[P[n] = (x[n])^2\]

This operation shifts all amplitude values to positive numbers representing instantaneous power, where a full-scale peak of \(\pm 1.0\) yields an energy value of \(1.0\).

4. Calculating the Mean (Average Power)

The sum of all squared samples within the block of length \(N\) is computed and divided by \(N\) to find the mean square value:

\[\text{MS} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} (x[n])^2\]

The mean square value (\(\text{MS}\)) represents the average electrical power of the audio segment over the duration \(T\).

5. Calculating the Root (RMS)

The square root of the mean square value is calculated to return the scale back to the original amplitude dimension:

\[x_{\text{RMS}} = \sqrt{\text{MS}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} (x[n])^2}\]

The resulting \(x_{\text{RMS}}\) is a linear value lying in the range \([0.0, 1.0]\) for signals that do not exceed 0 dBFS. A constant direct-current (DC) signal at maximum amplitude (\(1.0\)) yields an RMS of \(1.0\), whereas a full-scale sine wave yields:

\[x_{\text{RMS, full-scale sine}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \approx 0.7071\]

6. Logarithmic Conversion to dBFS

Human hearing perceives loudness logarithmically. To express the linear RMS value in decibels relative to full scale (dBFS), apply the base-10 logarithm scaled by a factor of 20:

\[L_{\text{RMS}} = 20 \log_{10}(x_{\text{RMS}})\]

Alternatively, this can be derived directly from the mean square power using a factor of 10:

\[L_{\text{RMS}} = 10 \log_{10}\left(\frac{1}{N} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} (x[n])^2\right)\]

In this scale, \(0 \text{ dBFS}\) is the maximum possible RMS level for a square wave at full scale, while a full-scale sine wave measures approximately \(-3.01 \text{ dBFS}\) under the AES17 definition without standard sine-wave calibration offsets.

7. Multichannel Considerations

For stereo or surround Opus streams, loudness can be calculated per channel or averaged across all channels. To calculate total unweighted RMS across \(M\) channels:

\[x_{\text{RMS, total}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{M \cdot N} \sum_{m=1}^{M} \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} (x_m[n])^2}\]

This calculation treats each channel with equal weight, deriving an aggregate signal power before converting to decibels.
