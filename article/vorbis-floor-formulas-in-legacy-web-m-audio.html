Vorbis Floor Formulas in Legacy WebM Audio

Before the widespread adoption of Opus, early implementations of the WebM multimedia container paired VP8 video with Vorbis audio. Within the Vorbis codec, the "floor" represents the coarse spectral energy envelope applied across frequency bins to synthesize modified discrete cosine transform (MDCT) audio frames. The Vorbis specification defines two distinct mathematical models for decoding these floor curves: Floor 0, which uses Line Spectral Pairs (LSP) mapped over the Bark psychoacoustic scale, and Floor 1, which utilizes piecewise linear interpolation in a quantized decibel domain.

Spectral Floor Purpose in Vorbis

The spectral floor \(F[k]\) acts as a synthesis curve applied to MDCT residue values \(R[k]\) across frequency bin indices \(k \in [0, N/2 - 1]\) (where \(N\) is the MDCT window length). The final reconstructed spectral line \(X[k]\) is calculated as:

\[X[k] = R[k] \cdot F[k]\]

Floor 1: Piecewise Linear dB Curves

Floor 1 is the primary floor model found in production WebM audio streams. It represents the envelope as a sequence of sorted two-dimensional coordinate pairs (knots) defined by frequency position \(X_i\) and quantized amplitude \(Y_i\).

1. Integer-Based Linear Interpolation

For any MDCT bin index \(x\) situated between adjacent knot positions \(X_A\) and \(X_B\) such that \(X_A \le x < X_B\), the interpolated floor value \(y(x)\) is calculated using integer arithmetic to guarantee bit-exact cross-platform decoding:

\[y(x) = Y_A + \left\lfloor (Y_B - Y_A) \cdot \frac{x - X_A}{X_B - X_A} \right\rfloor\]

2. Amplitude Conversion to Linear Domain

The interpolated value \(y(x)\) is a scalar representing energy in a decibel-like space. Vorbis decodes this value to linear gain through a lookup table representing the following exponential decay function:

\[F_1(x) = \text{inverse\_dB\_table}[y(x)]\]

Mathematically, this transformation corresponds to:

\[F_1(x) \approx 10^{\frac{y(x) - \text{offset}}{20 \cdot \text{scale}}}\]

In the standard Vorbis specification, this is precalculated as:

\[F_1(x) = \exp\left( \frac{y(x) \cdot \ln(10) \cdot \text{multiplier}}{20} \right)\]

where \(y(x)\) maps from an integer index (typically bounded between \(0\) and \(255\)) into a normalized linear amplitude multiplier bounded in \((0.0, 1.0]\).

Floor 0: Line Spectral Pair (LSP) Synthesis

Floor 0 utilizes linear predictive coding parameters mapped into Line Spectral Pairs (LSPs) across non-linear critical frequency bands.

1. Bark Scale Mapping

Linear MDCT bin indices \(k\) at sample rate \(f_s\) are mapped to a continuous Bark psychoacoustic frequency approximation \(b\):

\[f = \frac{k \cdot f_s}{N}\]

\[\text{Bark}(f) = 13.1 \arctan(0.00076 f) + 2.24 \arctan\left(\left(\frac{f}{7500}\right)^2\right) + 0.0001 f\]

2. LSP Filter Evaluation

Given \(M\) line spectral frequencies \(\{\omega_0, \omega_1, \dots, \omega_{M-1}\}\) decoded from the bitstream, the normalized angular frequency \(\omega\) is derived from the mapped Bark value:

\[\omega = \pi \cdot \frac{\text{Bark}(f)}{\text{Bark}(f_s / 2)}\]

The prediction filter polynomial \(P(\omega)\) is evaluated by separating even and odd LSP roots:

\[P(\omega) = \frac{1}{2} \left[ \prod_{m=0, 2, 4 \dots}^{M-2} \left( \cos\omega - \cos\omega_m \right)^2 + \prod_{m=1, 3, 5 \dots}^{M-1} \left( \cos\omega - \cos\omega_m \right)^2 \right]\]

3. Linear Envelope Extraction

The scalar gain multiplier \(A\), decoded as an overall frame amplitude, is combined with the LSP envelope to produce the final curve value:

\[F_0(k) = \exp\left( \frac{\text{scale} \cdot A}{\sqrt{P(\omega)}} \right)\]

Due to high computational requirements and precision differences across architectures, Floor 0 is rarely used in contemporary legacy WebM files, making the Floor 1 piecewise formulation the standard path inside Vorbis decoders.
