How VP9 Calculates SSIM in WebM Optimization

During multi-pass WebM encoding, the VP9 codec calculates the Structural Similarity Index (SSIM) to evaluate perceived video quality and guide rate-distortion optimization (RDO). Unlike traditional metrics like Mean Squared Error (MSE) or Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), which only quantify absolute pixel differences, SSIM measures degradation in structural information, luminance, and contrast. By calculating local SSIM values across blocks and frames in analysis and encoding passes, VP9 dynamically allocates bitrate, adjusts quantization parameters (QP), and selects optimal prediction modes to maximize human visual perception.

The Mathematical Foundation of SSIM in VP9

The standard SSIM metric evaluates two image windows—the original source frame block (\(x\)) and the reconstructed/compressed block (\(y\))—across three core visual components:

  1. Luminance (\(l\)): Measures the closeness of mean pixel intensities. \[\mu_x = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N x_i, \quad \mu_y = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^N y_i\] \[l(x, y) = \frac{2\mu_x\mu_y + C_1}{\mu_x^2 + \mu_y^2 + C_1}\]

  2. Contrast (\(c\)): Measures the standard deviations of pixel intensities. \[\sigma_x^2 = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - \mu_x)^2, \quad \sigma_y^2 = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^N (y_i - \mu_y)^2\] \[c(x, y) = \frac{2\sigma_x\sigma_y + C_2}{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2 + C_2}\]

  3. Structure (\(s\)): Measures the cross-correlation after luminance and contrast normalization. \[\sigma_{xy} = \frac{1}{N-1}\sum_{i=1}^N (x_i - \mu_x)(y_i - \mu_y)\] \[s(x, y) = \frac{\sigma_{xy} + C_3}{\sigma_x\sigma_y + C_3}\]

Setting \(C_3 = C_2 / 2\) combines these terms into the standard equation: \[\text{SSIM}(x, y) = \frac{(2\mu_x\mu_y + C_1)(2\sigma_{xy} + C_2)}{(\mu_x^2 + \mu_y^2 + C_1)(\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2 + C_2)}\]

The constants \(C_1 = (K_1 L)^2\) and \(C_2 = (K_2 L)^2\) prevent division by zero in near-flat regions, where \(L\) is the dynamic range of pixel values (255 for 8-bit video) and default scaling factors are typically \(K_1 = 0.01\) and \(K_2 = 0.03\).

Block-Level and Windowed Execution in libvpx

In the reference VP9 implementation (libvpx), SSIM calculations operate primarily on the luma (Y) plane, though chroma (U/V) components can also be incorporated.

  • Local Window Sampling: The calculation applies an \(8 \times 8\) or \(4 \times 4\) sliding pixel window across macroblocks and superblocks (from \(8 \times 8\) up to \(64 \times 64\) partitions).
  • Parallel Summation: The encoder computes intermediate sum values in parallel using SIMD instructions (AVX2, SSE4.1, ARM NEON). It tracks five localized sums for each window: \(\sum x\), \(\sum y\), \(\sum x^2\), \(\sum y^2\), and \(\sum xy\).
  • Mean SSIM (MSSIM): The global frame-level quality score is calculated by averaging local SSIM values across all sliding windows: \[\text{MSSIM} = \frac{1}{M}\sum_{j=1}^M \text{SSIM}_j\]

Integration into Two-Pass Optimization

VP9 calculates SSIM differently across its multi-pass encoding pipeline to optimize bitrate and visual quality.

Pass 1: Structural Complexity Profiling

During the first pass, VP9 analyzes frame sequences with a low-complexity pass to compute variance, motion vectors, and frame-level structural entropy. This generates statistical logs (stats.log) containing spatial and temporal complexity metrics for every frame.

Pass 2: Rate-Distortion Optimization (RDO) and Tuning

When configured with --tune=ssim, VP9 replaces standard Mean Squared Error with an SSIM-derived distortion metric inside its rate-distortion decision loops:

  • Lagrangian Cost Function: Mode selection, block partitioning (\(64 \times 64\) down to \(4 \times 4\)), and transform sizes are determined by minimizing: \[J = D_{\text{SSIM}} + \lambda R\] where \(D_{\text{SSIM}} = 1 - \text{SSIM}\), \(R\) is the estimated bit cost, and \(\lambda\) is the Lagrange multiplier linked to the frame QP.
  • Adaptive Quantization (AQ-Mode): When SSIM-guided adaptive quantization is active, VP9 shifts the quantization index (delta_q) per superblock:
    • Flat/Smooth Regions: Human vision easily detects compression artifacts in uniform areas. SSIM detects lower contrast variance (\(\sigma_x^2\)), prompting the encoder to lower QP and preserve gradients.
    • Textured/Complex Regions: High-variance regions mask distortion visually. The encoder increases QP slightly, reclaiming bits without significantly reducing perceptual SSIM.

Speed and Computational Optimizations

To reduce the computational overhead of computing full windowed SSIM during exhaustive mode evaluation, VP9 employs fast approximation paths:

  • Integer Arithmetic: Real-time and fast encoding presets use scaled integer representations to calculate variances and covariance without floating-point division.
  • Downsampled Evaluation: Fast modes may subsample pixels or evaluate SSIM only on representative sub-blocks instead of continuous overlapping windows.
  • Transform-Domain Distortion: For early skip decisions, the encoder compares discrete cosine transform (DCT) or asymmetric discrete sine transform (ADST) coefficients as proxies for structural preservation before committing to full pixel-domain SSIM reconstruction.
