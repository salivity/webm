WebM vs JPEG Video: Ringing Artifacts Around Text

Ringing artifacts, visible as halo-like distortions or "mosquito noise" near high-contrast text edges, differ substantially between WebM and JPEG-based video formats due to their underlying compression algorithms. While Motion JPEG (MJPEG) relies on rigid, frame-by-frame 8x8 Discrete Cosine Transforms (DCT) that produce static, block-confined ripples around letters, modern WebM codecs (VP8, VP9, and AV1) utilize variable block sizes, directional intra-prediction, and advanced in-loop deblocking filters to suppress or diffuse these artifacts far more effectively at equivalent bitrates.

Causes of Ringing on Sharp Text Edges

Sharp text edges introduce abrupt transitions in pixel brightness, creating high-frequency spatial components. Lossy compression algorithms quantize and discard these high frequencies to reduce file size. When the inverse transform reconstructs the image, this loss causes mathematical oscillations known as the Gibbs phenomenon. Visually, these oscillations manifest as faint, wavy outlines or blur surrounding the sharp perimeter of the letters.

Ringing in JPEG-Based Video (MJPEG)

Motion JPEG compresses every frame independently using standard JPEG encoding:

  • Fixed 8x8 DCT Blocks: JPEG splits images into uniform 8x8 pixel blocks. When text spans across or ends within an 8x8 block, high-frequency quantization causes ringing strictly within that boundary, leading to a pixelated "buzzing" around letter strokes.
  • Absence of In-Loop Filtering: Standard JPEG lacks post-quantization deblocking or deringing filters. Consequently, ringing ripples remain sharp and prominent against flat backgrounds.
  • Chroma Subsampling Issues: MJPEG commonly uses 4:2:0 or 4:2:2 chroma subsampling without advanced edge-adaptation, which creates color bleeding and color-specific ringing along colored text edges.

Ringing in WebM (VP8, VP9, AV1)

WebM files typically encapsulate video encoded with VP8, VP9, or AV1, which handle spatial frequencies with modern transform techniques:

  • Variable Transform Sizes: Unlike MJPEG’s fixed 8x8 grid, VP9 and AV1 support transform sizes ranging from 4x4 up to 64x64, as well as non-square transforms. Smaller blocks (4x4) can isolate sharp text edges, minimizing the spatial spread of ringing.
  • Directional Intra-Prediction: WebM codecs predict spatial patterns along specific angles before applying transforms. This prediction flattens the residual error near straight text strokes, significantly lowering the energy that leads to ringing.
  • In-Loop Deblocking and Deringing: WebM codecs incorporate built-in loop filters. For instance, AV1 uses the Constrained Directional Enhancement Filter (CDEF), which specifically identifies edge directions and smoothes ringing oscillations along those edges without blurring the primary stroke.

Direct Comparison Summary

  • Visual Appearance: MJPEG produces distinct, high-contrast halos strictly bound to 8x8 block grids. WebM produces much softer, less noticeable ripples that are often smoothed away entirely by loop filters.
  • Temporal Stability: MJPEG ringing remains static if the text is static. In WebM, inter-frame prediction can sometimes cause ringing to subtly flicker over time ("mosquito noise") at very low bitrates, though it remains far less severe overall.
  • Compression Efficiency: To achieve clean, readable text free of ringing, MJPEG requires exceptionally high bitrates. WebM codecs deliver crisp, ringing-free text at a fraction of the bandwidth required by JPEG-based video.
